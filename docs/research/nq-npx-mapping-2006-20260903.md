## Critical correction: old nine-series PIT sample is superseded

The earlier N-Q segmentation treated every `SCHEDULE OF INVESTMENTS (CONTINUED)` marker as a separate candidate portfolio and then assigned that page to a series using token overlap inside the holdings body. This was structurally wrong.

The assignment audit proved that continuation pages could be mapped to an unrelated ETF when an industry word happened to overlap its name. Examples included:
- a software continuation page being assigned to SPDR Biotech ETF;
- the actual Biotech page then being displaced by the larger false candidate;
- analogous misassignment around Homebuilders and other streetTRACKS series.

Therefore all mapping results based on the old nine-series PIT artifact `9854510485`, including the previously reported **43.53% count / 60.67% weight** frozen-master result, are retained only as mapping-engine history. They are **not valid evidence of historical-universe quality** and must not be used as the active baseline.

No strategy returns were inspected in discovering or correcting this issue.

## Corrected schedule-to-series rule

Implemented in `scripts/research-nq-series-segmentation-2006.py`.

New rule:
1. identify each schedule page from the nearest exact filing-time registered ETF series title around the schedule marker;
2. never use holdings/industry-word similarity to determine series identity;
3. assign continuation pages to that explicit series identity;
4. concatenate all pages belonging to the same series before parsing holdings;
5. for the final series in a filing, stop at the first explicit `NET ASSETS ... 100%` boundary so later filing tables cannot bleed into holdings;
6. only after grouping/parsing apply the structural eligibility rules.

The old `map_schedule_to_series()` token-overlap function remains only as a deprecated diagnostic compatibility function and must not be used to construct PIT holdings.

## Legacy `EC` / `US` structural diagnostic

Workflow: `Research NQ Legacy EC US Diagnostic 2006`
Run: `33714585732`
Artifact: `9878045597`

Thirty corrected explicit-series schedules were examined, including series that do not pass the final 10–120/top10 gate, to assess source structure without returns.

### EC analogue

- 28 / 30 series explicitly print a `COMMON STOCK(S/SHARES) -- xx%` allocation.
- Examples: XLE 99.8%, XBI 100.0%, XSD 100.0%, most sector/style ETFs 99.5–100.0%.
- Short-term investments are generally printed separately.

Conclusion: N-Q contains strong schedule-level structural evidence for equity-class exposure. A legacy `EC` bridge is plausible, but the current percentage is portfolio-section evidence; it is not yet a validated per-holding replacement for N-PORT `ASSET_CAT=EC`.

### US analogue

Country cannot be inferred safely from the absence of a country heading.

Only one examined series had explicit country allocation headings: `streetTRACKS DJ Global Titans ETF` (DGT), including:
- United States: **62.7%**
- United Kingdom: 17.7%
- Switzerland: 7.1%
- Japan: 2.6%
- France: 2.3%
- and smaller country allocations.

DGT also contained numerous ADR/GDR references. MTK contained ADR references despite having no explicit country-allocation section.

Conclusion: **do not default country-unknown holdings to US.** Explicit country headings can be used when present; ADR/GDR is a useful foreign-security flag but is not sufficient by itself to reproduce `INVESTMENT_COUNTRY=US` for all holdings. US parity remains an active gate.
