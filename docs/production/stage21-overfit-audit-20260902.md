# Stage21 Overfit / Selection-Bias Audit — 2026-09-02

## Scope
Audit `momentum-stage21-sbi-2026-09-v1` for parameter overfit and research-selection bias. This document does not change any production rule.

## Findings

### 1. Local parameter overfit risk: LOW to MODERATE
Evidence against a narrow optimum:
- Stage21 rounded-v1 preserved the result after replacing fractional midpoint weights by simple 0.5%-rounded funded weights.
- A preregistered coarse neighborhood test produced 6 PASS results out of 7 nearby configurations. The single failure was the higher-risk Normal 90% Fixed60 endpoint, which missed the historical DD gate rather than showing a sharp return collapse.
- Yellow/Deep funded allocations tolerated +/-1 percentage-point perturbations with very small changes in planning proxy and MaxDD.
- Additional dense weight, threshold and lookback tuning was explicitly stopped.

Interpretation: Stage21 is not supported by a single fragile decimal-weight optimum.

### 2. Rule-mining / threshold overfit risk: MODERATE
The frozen research process contains several protections:
- Stage8 DBMF used only 0/10/15/20 preregistered points; no intermediate grid.
- Stage10 BTAL/DBMF used only 0/50/100 substitution; no finer grid.
- Stage15 Yellow was the exact arithmetic midpoint rather than a fitted new weight.
- CFTC four-report lookback, participant class, magnitude threshold, VIX/OAS/put-call thresholds and M3 thresholds were explicitly prohibited from further tuning after their respective tests.

However, the final architecture still emerged after many distinct architecture families were compared on the same 2020-2026 history. This creates model-selection bias even when each individual stage is sparsely parameterized.

### 3. Multiple-testing / selection-bias stress: SURVIVES, but is not proof
Using the frozen-sample daily equity series through 2026-08-25, the observed annualized Sharpe is about 1.71. Daily returns are non-normal (skew about 0.73; kurtosis about 12.37), so a non-normal probabilistic-Sharpe adjustment was used.

Because the exact effective number of independent research trials is unknowable, a conservative DSR-style stress was evaluated for hypothetical independent trial counts:

| assumed trials | expected best-null annual Sharpe hurdle | DSR-style probability observed Sharpe exceeds hurdle |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 0.74 | 99.5% |
| 40 | 0.85 | 98.8% |
| 80 | 0.95 | 97.7% |
| 160 | 1.05 | 96.0% |

This indicates that the raw risk-adjusted performance is large enough that simple multiple-testing alone does not explain it, even under aggressive trial-count assumptions.

Important limitation: this is a DSR-style stress, not a canonical Deflated Sharpe Ratio calculation, because the true cross-trial Sharpe dispersion and dependence structure are not available. The research variants are also strongly correlated, so “number of independent trials” cannot be measured exactly.

### 4. Chronological robustness: SUPPORTIVE
Existing contiguous-window tests preserve chronology rather than shuffling momentum returns:
- 24M windows: 62.7% jointly pass CAGR>=40% and DD<=17%.
- 30M: 63.2%.
- 36M: 74.7%.
- 48M: 89.7%.
- Historical DD stayed inside the 17% target across the tested rolling windows, while CAGR varied materially.

This argues against the final result being generated by one short isolated episode, but it also shows that CAGR>=40% is not stable over 2-3 year horizons.

### 5. Implementation robustness: SUPPORTIVE
The strategy survived:
- materially higher transaction-cost stresses, including 100bp/side;
- +3 and +5 session timing delays without collapse;
- whole-share next-open account simulations from $10k to $250k;
- actual CFTC release-date correction for the 2025 government-shutdown backlog.

Ablation also showed interpretable component roles: removing M3 or CFTC materially worsened DD, while replacing GLDM with Cash mainly reduced return density and slightly worsened the DD gate. This is preferable to a strategy whose result disappears when one arbitrary parameter is removed.

## Overall assessment

- **Point-parameter overfit:** not strongly indicated.
- **Architecture-selection bias:** definitely present, because Stage21 was selected after many same-sample architecture experiments.
- **Evidence that the entire historical result is a data-mined accident:** not strong; plateau, chronology-preserving windows, cost/delay tests, ablations and DSR-style multiple-testing stress all remain supportive.
- **Can same-sample analysis prove absence of overfit?** No.

The residual risk that cannot be removed statistically is research selection bias from repeatedly consulting 2020-2026 history. The only clean discriminator from this point is the already-frozen True Forward OOS series beginning 2026-09-02.

## Governance consequence
Do not change Stage21 weights, CFTC lookback/category, M3 thresholds, GLDM allocation or Fixed60 inner mechanics in response to historical re-analysis. Any such rule change must use a new strategy ID and restart the OOS clock.
